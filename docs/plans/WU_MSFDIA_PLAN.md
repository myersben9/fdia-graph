# Plan: the [WU26] attack as the optimization it is, and one home for attack generation

**Status: proposed.**

[WU26] defines its multi-snapshot attack (MS-FDIA) as an optimization: eq. (12) minimizes the
number of tampered measurements subject to the AC measurement model (13)-(20), the operating limits
(21)-(23) and a line-overload goal reached over several snapshots (24)-(25). The attack is the
solution of that problem.

The generator does not solve it today. It builds local false states that satisfy (13)-(18) and (21)-(23), with
targets drawn at random and no minimization, and it has no overload goal. The docs nevertheless cite
[WU26] as the source of the stealthy families. This plan closes the gap: the stealthy families are
generated by solving the paper's problem. It also moves every piece of attack generation into the
engine's attack mixin, so a reader finds all of it in one place.

## 1. What the generator does today, equation by equation

| [WU26] | today | where |
|---|---|---|
| (12) minimize the number of tampered measurements | not solved. Targets are drawn at random; the attack area is every bus within `hops` branches of them, and whatever meters that area touches are tampered. | `engine/physics.py: local_region`, `engine/records.py: _stealthy_frame` |
| (13)-(16) SCADA injections and flows consistent with the false state | satisfied exactly: the false state is a local AC solution, and the attack vector is `h(x_false) - h(x_true)` | `formulas/network.py: local_ac_solve`, `engine/records.py: _attack_vector` |
| (17)-(18) PMU voltage magnitude and angle | satisfied by the same attack vector | `_attack_vector` |
| (19)-(20) PMU branch current phasors | not modeled: the meter plan has no current-phasor channels | `engine/core.py: _meter_plan` |
| (21) voltage limits, (22)-(23) generator limits | checked; a false state outside them is halved or rejected | `formulas/attacks.py: within_limits` |
| (24)-(25) a line's flow driven to its limit over the window | not modeled. `Al` steers a line the other way (a real overload reads lighter), and the IEEE cases carry no usable line ratings (D3) | `engine/attacks.py: lra_delta` |
| the window t = κ ... κ+T | `At` and `Am` hold one attack area and target set over an episode and solve each frame on its own | `timeline.py: _ramp_episode, _am_episode` |

The random draw and the missing objective were deliberate simplifications when the local false
states were introduced (#98). They were never stated next to the citation. Step 1 of the sequence
fixes the wording before any code changes.

## 2. The problem, in the package's terms

For one episode: an attack area A (a connected subnetwork), a target line l with limit `S_max`,
snapshots t = κ ... κ+T, and the true states `x_t` and scans `z_t` from the timeline.

- **Variables:** the false state `x^a_t` of each snapshot: `|V|` and `θ` of buses in A; every
  other bus keeps its true voltage, as today.
- **Attack vector:** `a_t = h(x^a_t) - h(x_t)` on the metered channels, SCADA and PMU. Constraints
  (13)-(18) hold by construction, since they state that the tampered readings are the readings of
  the false state; (19)-(20) hold the same way once the meter plan has current-phasor channels
  (D4).
- **Objective (12):** the number of meters tampered over the window. Following the paper's
  experiments section, a change smaller than the meter's noise does not count:

      minimize  | { m : max_t |a_{m,t}| > σ_m } |

  The paper writes `||Σ_t (z^a_t - h_A(x^a_t))||_0` and calls it "the number of nonzero entries
  in the attack vector"; its results count compromised devices over the window, which is the set
  above. Decision D1 confirms this reading.
- **Constraints:** (21)-(23) on every snapshot, as today, and the goal (24)-(25): the flow magnitude
  on line l, as the tampered measurements report it, rises from `S_{l,κ}` and reaches `S_max` by
  κ+T. Stealth bound: each tampered meter's change from one snapshot to the next is at most its
  noise standard deviation `σ_m`, so a single snapshot shows the residual test noise only (the
  paper keeps per-snapshot magnitudes small for this reason).

## 3. How it is solved

The paper does not name its solver; it reports 1.1 to 7.5 s per snapshot. The plan solves the
problem exactly where it is small and by a bounded search where it is not, and records which.

**The structure that makes it tractable.** Because every tampered reading is a reading of the false
state, a meter can be tampered only when the state of a bus it depends on moves, and it counts
when its reading moves by more than its noise. So the objective is bounded by the support S (the buses of A whose voltage moves): the
injections at S and its neighbours, the flows on branches touching S, and the PMU readings at S.
Minimizing (12) is minimizing that count over the supports S for which a feasible false state
exists: a local AC solution with only S free that meets the goal and (21)-(23). Feasibility for one
S is the existing `local_ac_solve` with the goal added as a target; the cost of a solved S is
counted exactly, noise threshold included.

**The search.**

1. Branch and bound over connected supports containing the goal's buses. Each candidate has a
   cheap lower bound on its cost (the meters that must move when its buses move) and, once solved,
   an exact cost (the meters whose readings move beyond their noise). Candidates are taken in order
   of the lower bound; the search stops when the next lower bound is no smaller than the best exact
   cost found, and that best support is optimal.
2. On IEEE-14 and small areas the enumeration is exhaustive (an area of about ten buses has at
   most 2^10 supports, each a small Newton solve).
3. On IEEE-118 and 300, best-first search with a node budget. The file records per episode whether
   the optimum was proven or the best support within the budget was taken.

**Checks on the solver.** A brute-force test on IEEE-14 areas confirms the search returns the true
minimum. A continuous cross-check, iteratively reweighted ℓ1 on the local AC equations, runs on the
same cases and may never beat the search.

**Rejected:** a mixed-integer nonlinear program (binary per meter, big-M, nonconvex AC) handed to a
general solver. It needs a heavy optional dependency, gives no better guarantee than exhaustive
search on the small cases, and is slower on the large ones. It can serve as an offline validation
if a reviewer asks.

**The window.** Snapshots are solved in order, as the paper does. Snapshot t targets
`S_{l,t} >= S_{l,κ} + (t - κ)/T · (S_max - S_{l,κ})`, starts its search from the previous
snapshot's support, and keeps each meter's change from the previous snapshot under the stealth
bound. The support may shrink over the window, as the paper's Fig. 4 reports.

## 4. What changes for each family

- **`Am` becomes the MS-FDIA of [WU26]:** the overload goal over the window, minimum tampering.
- **`Aq`, `At` and `Al`** keep their goals (a load step, a slow ramp, a masked overload) but are
  generated by the same minimizer: the least-tampering local false state that reaches the family's
  goal, instead of a random area. Decision D2.
- **`Ad`, `As`, `Ar`** (in-place corruption) are not [WU26] attacks and do not change.

## 5. One home for attack generation

The engine's attack mixin is meant to hold how attacks are built. Today the logic is spread over
five modules:

| module | attack logic in it today |
|---|---|
| `engine/attacks.py` (`AttackMixin`) | the in-place corruptions; the `Al` target line and redistribution |
| `engine/records.py` | every frame builder (`_stealthy_frame`, `_resolve_frame`, `_lra_frame`, `_am_frame`, `_corrupt_frame`), the false state and its limit check, the attack vector, the tamper set, the halving retries |
| `engine/physics.py` (`PhysicsMixin`) | the attacker's area (`local_region`, zero-injection growth) and the local false-state solve (`solve_local`) |
| `timeline.py` | each episode's attack design: ramp draws, target picking, the `Am` shape, its held redistribution and its peak check |
| `formulas/attacks.py`, `formulas/network.py` | the pure formulas (limits, ramp profile, subnetwork, local AC solve) |

Target layout:

- **`engine/attacks/`**, one package whose modules compose the one `AttackMixin`: `area.py` (the
  attack area), `false_state.py` (the local solve, the limits, the attack vector, the tamper set),
  `minimize.py` (the ℓ0 search), `stealthy.py` (the `Aq`/`At`/`Al`/`Am` frames and episode designs),
  `corrupt.py` (`Ad`/`As`/`Ar`). A reader looking for how an attack is made opens this package.
- **`engine/records.py`** keeps the benign frame and the replay memory, and asks the mixin for an
  attacked frame.
- **`timeline.py`** keeps when and where: the schedule, the episode placement, the storage. What
  an episode's attack is comes from the mixin.
- **`formulas/`** keeps the pure formulas, per the kernel rule; the mixin calls them.

This move comes first and changes no number: the strict frozen suite stays bit-exact, so the
minimizer lands in code already organised for it.

## 6. How we know it is right

- **The paper's IEEE-14 scenarios:** overloads on lines 3-4 and 6-11, and on 1-2 and 4-5. [WU26]
  reports tampered SCADA devices at buses 3, 4, 5, 6 and 11 with PMUs at 4 and 6, and a largest
  change near 0.22 pu, for the first. Our loads and noise differ, so the comparison is of the
  device set and magnitude, reported side by side, not bit for bit.
- **Optimality:** the brute-force test on IEEE-14 areas, and proven-versus-budget counts per system.
- **Stealth:** per snapshot, the residual test at the noise level sees noise only; each meter's
  per-snapshot change sits under the bound.
- **The goal:** the reported flow on the target line reaches `S_max` by the window's end.
- **Cost:** tampered meters per frame before and after (today's footprint is 3% to 45% of meters),
  and solve time per snapshot next to the paper's 1.1 to 7.5 s.

## 7. Data and documentation

The stealthy families change, so this needs a data release (v0.9.0): the frozen references of the
stealthy families are regenerated, and the guides' tables and the papers' numbers that use these
families are rerun. Until the release, the documentation describes the families as they are.

## 8. Sequence

1. **Documentation matches the code** (docs only): wherever the docs cite [WU26] for the stealthy
   families (`README.md`, `docs/guides/generation.md`, `docs/reference/CONCEPTS_TO_CODE.md`,
   `docs/reference/FORMULAS.md`, `docs/reference/GLOSSARY.md`), say they satisfy the paper's
   constraints (13)-(18) and (21)-(23), not its objective (12) or goal (24)-(25).
2. **One home for attack generation** (section 5): no behaviour change, bit-exact frozen suite.
3. **The ℓ0 minimizer** (section 3): the support search with its brute-force and reweighted-ℓ1
   tests, behind a generator knob that is off by default.
4. **The [WU26] attack:** line limits (D3), the overload goal and the window, `Am` as the MS-FDIA,
   the other stealthy families on the minimizer (D2), current-phasor meters if chosen (D4).
5. **Data release v0.9.0** with the IEEE-14 scenario comparison, the guides rerun and the claims
   updated to what is then true.

## 9. Open decisions

- **D1, the objective's reading.** The number of distinct meters tampered above their noise over
  the window (recommended, it is what the paper counts in its results), or the literal norm of the
  summed attack vector, which lets opposite changes cancel.
- **D2, which families use the minimizer.** `Am` only, or every stealthy family (recommended: the
  area of every stealthy attack is then the least a real attacker would need).
- **D3, where `S_max` comes from.** Pandapower's ratings cannot serve: every line and
  transformer of IEEE-14, 118 and 300 is rated 9,900 MVA, MATPOWER's placeholder for "no
  limit". The base case loads the most loaded line to 1.5% (IEEE-14), 4.5% (118) and 8.7% (300)
  of it, so an overload goal on those ratings could never be reached. Recommended: the branch
  ratings (`rate_a`) of the PGLib-OPF versions of the same cases, a published and citable source
  that pandapower reads with its MATPOWER converter, mapped to our branches. Alternative: a
  multiple of each line's largest flow over the operating pool.
- **D4, PMU current phasors (19)-(20).** Add branch current-phasor channels at PMU buses to the
  meter plan (a file-format change in the same release), or state the omission.
- **D5, the overload's meaning.** (24)-(25) constrain the flow the tampered measurements report,
  so the operator sees the line reach its limit. Whether the physical line then overloads depends
  on the operator's response, which the paper does not model. Recommended: implement the equations
  as written and say so.
