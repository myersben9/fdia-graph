    The attack families¶

    A timeline holds attacks so that a detector can be trained and scored on them. This page says what an attack in a timeline is, which families the generator makes, and which class produces each. It describes what the dataset contains. The equations are in Formulas, each with its source.

    Paths are under src/fdia_graph/.

    An episode¶

    An attack is an episode: a run of consecutive frames under one design. An episode has a family, an onset, a length and the buses it attacks. Episodes never overlap, so a frame has at most one active family. Every frame of an episode carries the attack, except a frame whose scan cannot be built: it is stored benign and counted in fallback_benign.

    The local false state¶

    Both families the generator makes are built on one idea, the local false state of [WU26].

    The attacker wants the operator to believe a state of the grid that is not the true one. A detector that checks the readings against the grid's equations would catch readings that fit no state. So the attacker picks a false state that the equations allow:

    1. Choose a small area of the grid: the buses within a few branches of the targets. A ramp has up to five targets, which need not be neighbours, so its area can be several neighbourhoods.
    2. Change the loads inside the area (and, for an overload, the generator outputs).
    3. Solve the AC equations of the area again, with every voltage outside it held true.
    4. Add the difference of the readings to the true scan.

    The attack vector is a = h(x_false) - h(x_true), where h is the measurement function (Meters and noise). It is zero on every meter the area does not reach. Each meter keeps its usual noise. So the observed scan looks like a normal noisy scan of the false state, and a residual test finds nothing wrong.

    A false state must also be plausible. Inside the buses it re-solves it stays within the bus voltage limits and the generator limits of the case [WU26, eqs. 21-23]. The buses one branch outside take up the change in their reported injection, so a generator there is held to its limits too: both families check the generators of the reach, the buses re-solved and the buses one branch outside. A state that shows a generator outside its limits anywhere on the reach is rejected.

    A bus reports one net injection, its load less its generation, and the model has no separate telemetry of a unit's output. A change of a unit's implied output inside the injection meter's error is therefore not visible. A limit counts as passed only when the implied output is outside it by more than that meter can resolve: the meter's accuracy at the true reading, as the package simulates it [ASP14], and never less than 0.001 MW. The tolerance follows the bus's reading, not the unit's size. The slack and a static generator on a load bus have no limits in the case, so they are never refused.

    [WU26] leaves tampering below the noise out of its tamper count (Sec. V-A). Applying that idea to a limit is this package's step, not the paper's. The two use different noise scales here: the limit uses the meter's simulated accuracy, which is the noise in the file, and the overload's tamper count uses the flat noise of the paper's case study.

    A bus with no load and no producing unit injects nothing, whether or not it has a shunt: a shunt is part of the grid's admittances, not an injection. No attack shows an injection at such a bus. An area that would have one on its boundary grows to take it in.

    The two families¶

    Family Code What the attacker does
    At, the slow ramp 5 a false load that moves away from the true load a little each frame (up or down), holds, and returns to one step away from it
    Am, the overload 7 the reported flow of a line rises to its rating

    At is hard to see because each step is small, so a detector that looks for jumps sees none. Its ramp profile is described in [DAT26], and the published attack it follows is in the citation list.

    Am is the multi-snapshot overload attack of [WU26, eqs. 24-25]. Over the window the reported flow of the target moves from its true value to its limit. The operator sees a line about to overload that is not.

    The designs¶

    A design decides what an episode changes on each frame. The package's designs are search-based, and they run on every test system: the IEEE cases and the New York case.

    At Am
    Family class SearchRamp SearchOverload
    Targets up to 5 loads one or two lines, at their ratings
    False state from a local AC solve in numpy a local AC solve in numpy
    Designed as the walk reaches it before its split is walked

    Every family answers the same question for the timeline: the episode of a window (EpisodeFamily.designed in engine/attacks/family.py). That is why one path generates every timeline (Generate a timeline).

    The designs read a scan's load and generation from its state. How a bus's injection divides into the two is the setting loads (How a pool's load is read).

    The search-based designs¶

    The designs are methods of EpisodeDesigns, the generator's episode_designs: ramp_design and am_overload_design (engine/attacks/episodes.py). Settings are in TimelineSettings (the tables).

    The fewest-tamper search. [WU26, eq. 12] asks for the attack that tampers the fewest devices. The search tries supports, the sets of buses the false state moves: the whole area first, then smaller ones, smallest first. It keeps the one whose attack changes the fewest devices beyond noise (engine/attacks/minimize.py). It solves at most min_budget supports per search.

    At.

    • The targets are up to 5 loads drawn at random among the stealthy loads: attackable loads that are not on a generator bus [BOY22]. An attackable load has nonzero active power, is not on the slack bus and is at most max_load_mw.
    • One load factor moves all of them: it rises ramp_rate per frame, holds, and returns at the same rate. The rise lasts 20 % to 45 % of the episode and the hold 0 % to 25 % (draw_ramp). The factor never falls below one ramp_rate, so every frame of the episode carries an attack: after the return the episode stays one step away from the true load until it ends, and a ramp with a long rise can end before its return is complete. A ramp goes up or down with equal chance.
    • With min_tamper (the default) the search keeps only supports on which no metered channel moves by more than its rated accuracy times stealth_scale between frames. With min_tamper off, or when the search finds no such support (the episode's record then shows -1 devices), the ramp runs on its whole area with no such bound.
    • A step with no local solution is halved, up to 6 times (STEP_HALVINGS).
    • A design is tested on every frame it will occupy before it is accepted, up to 40 draws (ONSET_DRAWS).

    Am.

    • The targets are lines that are rated, flow-metered, below their rating at every snapshot of the window, and near a load or a generator the attacker can move (eligible_lines). Each episode overloads n_lines lines, two by default as in the case studies of [WU26].
    • The ratings are 1.25 times each branch's peak true flow over the pool (rating_source="pool"). rating_source="pglib" uses the PGLib-OPF ratings instead, which exist for IEEE-14, 118 and 300; another case raises NoLineRatings. rating_source="delta" rates each line from its own window: its true flow at the window's last snapshot plus rating_delta (per unit of the system base).
    • The goal of each snapshot is a linear share of the way from the true flow to the rating, and the last snapshot reaches the rating (overload_goal).
    • The loads and generators of the support are free inside their limits. The load a bus shows changes by at most load_cap times its true load [YUA11].
    • A device counts as tampered when it moves beyond the noise of [WU26]'s own case studies: 0.03 p.u. on a SCADA channel and 0.01 p.u. on a PMU channel.
    • Nothing bounds how far Am moves a channel between snapshots, as in [WU26].
    • support_method="rref" picks the support by the row reduction of [WU26] instead of the search (engine/attacks/rref.py).

    A family of your own¶

    A source takes design families of your own, each in place of the search family of one name (Generate a timeline). The simplest is a subclass of AttackFamily (engine/attacks/family.py). It returns an Episode (models/plan.py) from design(window, rng), or None when the window holds no attack. The window is the true state over the frames the episode would cover. The episode holds one attack vector: a value for every meter on every frame. The walk adds each row to the true scan of its frame (VectorEpisode).

    A design that finds no attack can say why (design_explained): a reason the family sets in last_miss, solver_error when the family counted a failed solve, else infeasible. Add a design family, an alarm rule or a test system has the steps.

    Labels and the tamper masks¶

    In the file Shape Meaning
    data/y [T, N] 1 at each attacked bus
    data/family [T] the family code; 0 is benign
    data/seq_id [T] the episode's row; -1 is benign
    attack/node_tamper, attack/edge_tamper as the readings 1 at a tampered meter
    attack/pmu_i_tamper as the currents 1 at a tampered PMU current
    episodes/ one row per episode onset, length, family, buses

    What a 1 in a tamper mask means depends on the family that designed the episode.

    Family A meter is tampered A bus is attacked
    SearchRamp, SearchOverload where the attack changed the stored reading at the buses the design names
    a family of your own that designs an attack vector where the vector moves the reading by more than the meter's accuracy class where a tampered meter sits at the bus

    The file's attribute tamper, on attack/, states the rule of the package's own families, and both rules when the source was given a family of your own.

    In every file the observed scan minus the benign scan is the attack, exactly (Layers and labels).

    What the released files additionally contain¶

    The released files of data release v0.8.3 hold seven families. The current generator makes two.

    Code Family In released files Generated today
    1 Aq, stealthy load scaling yes no
    2 Ad, meter corruption yes no
    3 As, meter scaling yes no
    4 Ar, replay yes no
    5 At, slow ramp yes yes
    6 Al, load redistribution yes no
    7 Am yes, as a load redistribution yes, as the overload

    The older families are read-only. Their codes stay in the label table fg.FAMILIES, and the files that carry them load, filter and score by name:

    import fdia_graph as fg

old = fg.load("ieee14", release="v0.8.3", families=["Aq"])

    The generator refuses them. In those files Aq, Al, Ad, As and Ar are one frame each, and the Am episodes drive no line to its limit. Those files also have an angle reading at every voltmeter bus and no PMU currents (Meters and noise). fg.STEALTHY_FAMILIES is the set of codes that are local false states: Aq, At, Al and Am.
