    Follow one attack through the code¶

    This page follows one overload (Am) episode on IEEE-14 from the pool of operating points to the frame in the file. Each step names the class and the method that does it, the file they are in, and what they produced for this episode. Read it with the code open beside it.

    The terms area, support, boundary, reach and snapshot are used as the Glossary defines them. Paths are under src/fdia_graph.

    Run it¶

    Everything below is printed by one script, examples/tour.py. It takes about a minute.

    pip install -e ".[generate]"
python examples/tour.py

    It generates a small timeline by the public call, then rebuilds the file's one episode by hand. The timeline is registered as tour in the local cache and replaces a dataset of that name:

    fg.generate("ieee14", name="tour", frames=600, seed=123, families=("Am",), attacked_frac=0.2)

    This is what it prints. Buses and branches are positions, counted from zero. On IEEE-14 the bus number is the position plus one.

    The sections below take the numbered lines in order. tests/test_tour.py runs the script and compares its output with the block above. That test runs only with FDIA_SLOW=1, and no scheduled workflow runs it, so run it after a change to the attack code.

    1. The source¶

    fg.generate (generation/__init__.py) reads the pool, keeps its first 600 frames and calls timeline.generate_timeline, which builds an IeeeSource (generation/ieee.py). IeeeSource is a SearchSource (generation/search.py) on an IEEE case by its name. The one driver of every timeline is TimelineSource.generate (generation/source.py): open the file, cut the splits, then split by split place the episodes, design them and walk the frames. Generate a timeline describes each of those steps.

    The script builds the same source a second time by hand, with the same pool, settings and seed, so that it can call the parts one by one.

    2. The case, the pool and the meters¶

    • The case. Network.from_ieee(14) (systems/network.py) holds the Case and its admittance matrices. FdiaGenerator (engine/core.py) reads it: 14 buses, 20 branches, the slack at position 0.
    • The pool. generation._load_states returns the operating points as an array [T, N, 4]: voltage magnitude, active injection, reactive injection and angle per bus. Each frame is one solved AC state. Injections are positive for load (NodeChannel, constants/channels.py).
    • The meter plan. MeterPlan.from_coverage (sensing/meters.py) draws the meters from the seed, at the coverage of MeterSettings, and places them with MeterPlan.from_placement. Here that is 89 meters: PMUs at positions 3 and 11, and a flow meter on 18 of the 20 branches. Only a PMU reads an angle [WU26 eqs. 17-20]. Meters and noise has the rules.
    • Who can be moved. FalseState.free_injection_buses (engine/attacks/false_state.py) lists the buses whose injection an overload attacker may change: the attackable loads, and the generators of FalseState.generator_buses [WU26 eqs. 13-14]. Position 6 injects nothing, so it is held at zero.

    SearchSource.__init__ also sets the line ratings before any frame is made (Overload.use_line_ratings, engine/attacks/overload.py). By default a branch's rating is rating_margin times its largest true flow over the pool the source was given, here the 600 frames.

    3. The window and the targets¶

    • The split. TimelineSource.cut_splits cuts the frames into train, validation and test in time order. Frame 92 is in the train split, code 0.
    • The onset. TimelineSource.place calls place_split (generation/plan.py), which draws each episode's onset from the generator's random stream. With attacked_frac=0.2 the train split gets one 60-frame episode and the two shorter splits none. The onset drawn is frame 92, so the window is frames 92 to 151.
    • The targets. SearchDesigner.task (generation/design.py) builds the episode's task, and Designer._design hands the window's 60 states to SearchOverload.designed (engine/attacks/search.py), which calls EpisodeDesigns.am_overload_design (engine/attacks/episodes.py). It does three things:
      1. Overload.eligible_lines keeps the branches that are rated, flow metered, below their rating at every snapshot, have both ends at the grid's own buses, and have a free injection in the area around their ends. Here 18 qualify.
      2. It puts them in a random order, drawn from a stream keyed by this episode, and Overload.target_sets forms pairs: a branch with the first other branch whose two ends lie inside its area.
      3. It tries each pair in turn, at most AM_LINE_TRIES of them, and keeps the first whose search (step 5) moves at least one device beyond noise.

    The pair kept is branches 10 and 11, which join position 8 to positions 9 and 13. The file records it in episodes/am_line, and the script reads it from there.

    4. The goal¶

    Overload.overload_goal builds a FlowGoal (models/frames.py): the apparent flow each target branch must show at each snapshot [WU26 eqs. 24-25]. The schedule is _schedule in engine/attacks/overload.py [WU26 eq. 25]. It is the true flow at that snapshot, plus a share of the gap between the last true flow and the rating. The share grows by one sixtieth per snapshot, so the last snapshot reaches the rating.

    The printed lines show it for both branches. On branch 10 the true flow is lower at the end of the window than at its start, while the goal climbs to the rating.

    5. The area, the support and the boundary¶

    Minimizer.min_tamper (engine/attacks/minimize.py) chooses where the attacker acts [WU26 eq. 12].

    • The area. Minimizer.window_area asks AttackArea.attack_area (engine/attacks/area.py), which under the default area_rule is AttackArea.hop_area: the buses within hops branches of the target branches' ends, the slack left out. With two hops on a grid this small, the area is every bus but the slack, and its boundary is the slack alone.
    • The candidates. The whole area is the first candidate. Minimizer._supports then yields smaller ones, smallest first: each starts from one end of each target branch and grows by one adjacent area bus at a time. A zero-injection bus on a candidate's boundary joins it, so a candidate can grow by more than one bus. A candidate must hold a free injection.
    • The cost. AttackWindow.cost (engine/attacks/window.py) solves the false state of a candidate at all 60 snapshots and returns a SupportCost: the devices and the channels moved beyond their meter's noise at some snapshot [WU26 eq. 28], and the candidate's size. The noise is each meter's accuracy at its true reading, the noise the file's readings carry. A candidate has no cost when its solve fails at any snapshot, when it moves no device beyond noise (an overload that no meter shows is no overload), or when it cannot beat the best found so far.
    • The result. The search solves at most min_budget candidates, 256 by default, and it used all of them here. The cheapest it found is the support of positions 9 and 13: 5 devices, against 11 for the whole area. The search makes no claim that this is the minimum.

    The boundary of that support is positions 8, 10 and 12: the buses one branch outside it. Their voltages stay true. The reach is the support with its boundary, the five buses whose reported injection the attack can change. The file records the support and the search's counts in the min_* columns of episodes/, and they match.

    6. The false state¶

    For each frame, FalseState.goal_state (engine/attacks/false_state.py) solves the false state of that snapshot's goal on the held support. For a flow goal it calls FalseState.flow_goal_state, which calls FalseState.solve_flow_local and gets a FlowSolve (models/frames.py):

    • Unknowns. The voltage magnitude and angle of the support's buses. Every other voltage is held at its true value. The printed line shows the voltage magnitude moving at positions 9 and 13 only.
    • What must hold. Each target branch shows its goal flow, by formulas.network.local_flow_solve. The free injections of the support change to make that so. Here both are loads, and FlowSolve.load_change is the load each one pretends.
    • Bounds. A load shows a change of at most load_cap times its true load [YUA11]. A generator stays inside its limits, to within what its bus's injection meter resolves, and a voltage inside its limit [WU26 eqs. 21-23]. A value that would pass a bound is pinned there and the solve repeated.
    • The last check. flow_goal_state then rejects a state that leaves the limits anywhere on the reach (formulas.attacks.within_limits).

    The pretended change is a few hundredths of a megawatt at the first snapshot and a few megawatts at the last, because the goal moves a little further from the truth at each snapshot.

    7. The frame and the meters it moves¶

    During the walk, Walk.episode (generation/emit.py) asks the episode for each frame. OverloadEpisode.frame calls Overload.overload_step, which solves the false state as in step 6 and hands it to AttackFrames.frame_from_state (engine/attacks/frames.py):

    1. Emitter.emit_from_state (engine/measurement.py) emits the true scan, with each meter's bias and this frame's noise. The noise is keyed by the frame's timestep, so the script sets g.scan_key to get the same draw.
    2. AttackFrames.attack_vector is the noiseless reading of the false state minus that of the true state [WU26 eqs. 13-18].
    3. The vector is added to the true scan on the node and flow meters it moves.
    4. The labels y are set at the free injections of the support, positions 9 and 13.
    5. AttackFrames.currents_with_attack does the same for the PMU currents, and the masks of every meter moved are returned as a MeterLayers (models/frames.py): node, flow and PMU current.

    Two counts appear on this page and they measure different things. The search's 5 devices are those moved beyond their meters' noise somewhere in the window. The masks mark every stored reading the attack changed in this frame, however little: 12 node channels across the reach, and both flow channels of the four metered branches with an end in the support. No PMU current is written, because neither PMU sits at an end of those branches.

    The frame built this way equals frame 151 of the file, value for value.

    8. The file¶

    TimelineWriter (generation/write.py) stores each frame as the walk produces it, and after the walk writes the tables. For this episode:

    In the file Holds
    data/node_x, data/edge_x, data/y the observed readings and the labels, one row per frame
    benign/, clean/ the same frames with the attack removed, and the noiseless truth
    attack/node_tamper, attack/edge_tamper, attack/pmu_i_tamper the masks of step 7
    episodes/onset, length, family, bus_idx the episode's row
    episodes/am_line, am_rating_mva, am_target_mva, am_reached_mva, am_emitted_mva one row per target branch
    episodes/min_devices, min_channels, min_evaluated, min_support_idx what the search chose

    The data dictionary describes the datasets every timeline holds, and its episode tables give the shape, type and meaning of each am_ and min_ column.

    fg.load("tour", order="time") reads the file back. ds.episodes is the episode table, and its buses are the buses the episode labelled: positions 9 and 13, the support's free injections.
